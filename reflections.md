# Reflections

## Interpretation

The experiment suggests that while the AI avoids overt bias in decision-making, there may be minor differences in confidence and tone depending on the name used.
This could have implications in real-world systems where even small differences in language may influence perception.

## Broader Implications
Even subtle differences in tone may influence human decision-making, especially in high-stakes areas like hiring. If AI systems consistently frame some candidates with more confidence than others, this could reinforce existing biases in indirect ways.

## Limitations
- The experiment used a small number of prompts
- Results may vary across different AI models
- Bias may not always be visible in short responses

## Future Work
- Test more diverse names and backgrounds
- Compare responses across multiple AI systems
- Explore ways to reduce or detect bias in outputs

## Final Reflection

This experiment shows that AI systems may appear neutral on the surface, but closer inspection reveals subtle differences in how individuals are described. Understanding and addressing these nuances is important for building fair and trustworthy AI systems.

I began by testing a simple question: does changing a name influence how an AI evaluates a candidate? At a surface level, the results suggest consistency the AI avoided making direct hiring decisions and emphasized the need for more information across all cases.

However, a closer examination reveals that neutrality in outcome does not necessarily mean neutrality in expression.

Subtle differences in tone, confidence, and framing emerged across responses. Some candidates were described with slightly more assertive or assumption-driven language, while others were evaluated more cautiously or conditionally. These variations, while small, are important. In real-world contexts such as hiring, even minor differences in how a candidate is described can influence human perception and decision-making.

This raises an important consideration: bias in AI systems is not always explicit or easily measurable. It can exist in nuanced forms in wording, emphasis, or implied confidence — making it harder to detect, but still impactful.

At the same time, this experiment highlights the complexity of evaluating AI behavior. The model demonstrated an overall cautious and balanced approach, suggesting that modern systems are designed to avoid overt bias. Yet, the presence of subtle variation indicates that fairness is not a binary state, but a spectrum.

Ultimately, this project reinforces the idea that building trustworthy AI systems requires more than preventing obvious harm. It requires careful attention to small details, the language, the framing, and the assumptions embedded in responses.

Understanding these nuances is essential if AI is to be used responsibly in high-stakes domains such as hiring, healthcare, and decision-making systems.
